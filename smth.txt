Touchless 3D CAD Model Navigation via Webcam Gesture Tracking for Engineering Laboratories

Topic: Touchless Air-gesture 3D CAD Controller
When engineering students work in labs, their hands often get dirty by the oil, solder, clay, and other chemicals. Every time they need to view their 3D design on a computer or laptop, they have to stop, and wash their hands, and use a mouse and keyboard. This project solves that problem by using a simple computer or laptop webcam to track hand movements in the air. The software converts mid-air gestures into computer commands, letting students rotate, move, and zoom in on their 3D models without ever touching the computer or smudging equipment. 

FEATURES:
Real-Time hand tracking: Uses a standard webcam to track 21 key points on a user’s hand instantly, without the needing of extra sensors.
Smart motion controls: It calculates the distance, and movement between fingers (like the index finger and thumb) to figure out how the 3D model should move.
Easy touchless gestures:
Pinch and move: Pinch fingers together and drag to slide the 3D model up, down, left, and right.
Open palm turning: Show an open palm to rotate 3D model freely in any direction
Two-Hand distance: Moving two hands closer  together or farther to zoom in, and out.
Smooth motion filter: It uses a built-in smoothing algorithm to prevent screen shaking or sudden jumps caused by small hand tremors.

OBJECTIVES:
To build a touchless input system using a standard webcam so engineering students can interact with software without dirtying physical keyboards and mouse.
To create an algorithm that converts hand gestures into CAD commands, translating actions like pinching, hand movement, and palm rotation into precise 3D model controls.
To measure the system’s speed, smoothness, and user efficiency in a real lab setting to prove it works as effectively as a traditional mouse.




RRL:
According to recent research on touchless computer interaction, using physical mouse and keyboards in shared spaces like laboratories leads to high rates of hardware wear and contamination. The study shows that touchless gesture controls offer a practical, hygienic alternative for operating computers in dirty or high-use environments. This directly supports our research by establishing the real-world necessity of our Touchless 3D CAD Controller, proving that hands-free controls protect university hardware from grease, solder, and chemicals while keeping student workflows uninterrupted.

According to Meng et al. (2024), Google MediaPipe tracks 21 key points on a hand using a standard laptop camera and automatically adjusts for changes in hand distance and angle. Their study shows that accurate, real-time hand controls do not require expensive depth cameras or special gloves. This directly supports our Touchless 3D CAD Controller by proving that a basic webcam is powerful enough to handle real-time 3D rotation and zooming smoothly. 

Meng, Y., Jiang, H., Duan, N., & Wen, H. (2024). Real-Time hand gesture monitoring model based on MediaPipe’s registerable system. Sensors, 24(19), 6262. https://doi.org/10.3390/s24196262

According to Tsai and Tsai (2021), achieving reliable hand gesture recognition in complex or noisy environments required a dedicated dual-camera hardware architecture with depth-mapping algorithms, and even then the system only reached an average accuracy of 83.98 percent. Their study shows that precision in gesture recognition is difficult to achieve even with specialized hardware built specifically for the task. This directly supports our research by highlighting the core technical challenge our project must overcome: achieving reliable gesture recognition accuracy using only a standard single-camera webcam, without the dual-camera depth-sensing setup their study relied on.
Tsai, T.-H., & Tsai, Y.-R. (2021). Architecture design and VLSI implementation of 3D hand gesture recognition system. Sensors, 21(20), Article 6724. https://doi.org/10.3390/s21206724
According to Friedrich et al. (2021), manipulating 3D objects with a 2D input device like a mouse requires a complex mental transfer that slows down and complicates the CAD modeling process. Their study proposes a system combining VR hand gestures with voice commands to manipulate CAD models, showing that gesture-based control can make 3D modeling more intuitive than traditional mouse input. This directly supports our Touchless 3D CAD Controller by confirming that gesture input addresses a real usability problem in CAD software, though their approach still depends on VR headset hardware rather than a webcam alone.
